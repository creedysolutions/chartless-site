What key is this song in?

Three ways to find out, from ear-only to let-the-phone-do-it.
Short answer: the key is where the song feels at home. Hum the last note of the chorus or find the final chord; that's usually the key. G, C, D and Em together almost always means the key of G.

1. Listen for home

Play the song and stop it at the end of a chorus. Hum the note that feels finished, like the song could end right there. Find that note on your instrument. That's your tonic, and the song is probably in that key.

2. Check the chords against the key families

If you have the chords (or can work out a few), match them to a key. Every major key has the same pattern: 1, 2m, 3m, 4, 5, 6m. A song using G, C, D, Em fits G (1, 4, 5, 6m). A song using A, D, E, F#m fits A. The Nashville number chart lists every key so you can look it up.

The chord the song keeps returning to, and usually ends on, is the 1.

Major or minor?

If home feels sad or dark, it's a minor key. Minor keys share chords with a major key (A minor uses the same chords as C major), so look at where the song resolves: on Am, it's A minor; on C, it's C major.

3. Let it hear it for you

Chartless listens to whatever is playing on your phone, tablet, Mac or in Chrome and shows the key, the chords and the Nashville numbers as the song goes. It's the fastest answer when a song changes key, has a capo'd guitar, or was recorded a little sharp. The live chart is free.
