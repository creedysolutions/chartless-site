How to transpose chords to any key

The singer wants it lower. Two steps, no music theory degree.
Short answer: find each chord's number in the original key, then read the same numbers in the new key. G C D Em in G is 1 4 5 6m. In A, 1 4 5 6m is A D E F#m. Same song, new key.

Step 1: write the chords as numbers

Find the original key in the left column, then find each chord in that row. The column header is its number. Chords that aren't in the row are usually borrowed; a ♭7 (F in the key of G) is the common one, and it's in the table too.

Step 2: read the numbers in the new key

Go to the new key's row and read across with the same numbers. Keep any extras the same: a G/B in G is 1/3, which is A/C# in A; a Csus2 becomes Dsus2.

Shortcuts worth knowing

  • Up a whole step (G to A, C to D): move every chord up two frets' worth: G→A, C→D, D→E, Em→F#m.
  • Guitarist wants the old shapes? Use a capo instead of new chords. The capo chart shows which fret.
  • Number charts never need transposing. That's why worship teams use Nashville numbers.
