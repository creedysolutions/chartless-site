Chords in the key of F♯

F♯ major. Relative minor: D♯m.

Six sharps, and yes, E♯ is a real note in this key. On guitar, capo and play E or D shapes. Your fretting hand will thank you.

The 1, 4 and 5 (F♯, B, C♯) are the backbone. Add the 6m (D♯m) and you can play most of what's on the radio. The 7° (E♯dim) mostly shows up as a passing chord, so don't lose sleep over it.

Progressions you'll actually run into

  • 1 5 6m 4 (The four-chord pop and worship progression): F♯ · C♯ · D♯m · B
  • 1 4 5 (Three-chord rock, folk, blues and old hymns): F♯ · B · C♯
  • 6m 4 1 5 (The same four chords starting on the minor): D♯m · B · F♯ · C♯
  • 2m 5 1 (The jazz turnaround): G♯m · C♯ · F♯
  • 1 6m 4 5 (The fifties doo-wop changes): F♯ · D♯m · B · C♯

Those numbers are the Nashville number system. Learn the progression as numbers once and it works in every key.

Playing in F♯ on guitar

Capo 2, play E shapes; capo 4, play D shapes; capo 6, play C shapes.

The capo chart has every key, and the transpose chart moves a song somewhere else entirely.

Neighbor keys
