Chords in the key of B♭

B♭ major. Relative minor: Gm.

Two flats. Another horn-section favorite, and on guitar a capo 1 with A shapes or capo 3 with G shapes does the job.

The 1, 4 and 5 (B♭, E♭, F) are the backbone. Add the 6m (Gm) and you can play most of what's on the radio. The 7° (Adim) mostly shows up as a passing chord, so don't lose sleep over it.

Progressions you'll actually run into

  • 1 5 6m 4 (The four-chord pop and worship progression): B♭ · F · Gm · E♭
  • 1 4 5 (Three-chord rock, folk, blues and old hymns): B♭ · E♭ · F
  • 6m 4 1 5 (The same four chords starting on the minor): Gm · E♭ · B♭ · F
  • 2m 5 1 (The jazz turnaround): Cm · F · B♭
  • 1 6m 4 5 (The fifties doo-wop changes): B♭ · Gm · E♭ · F

Those numbers are the Nashville number system. Learn the progression as numbers once and it works in every key.

Playing in B♭ on guitar

Capo 1, play A shapes; capo 3, play G shapes; capo 6, play E shapes.

The capo chart has every key, and the transpose chart moves a song somewhere else entirely.
