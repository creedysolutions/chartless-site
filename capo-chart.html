Capo chart: what key you're in for every capo fret

Each fret up is one half step. Play the same shapes, sound in a new key.
Short answer: a capo raises every chord one half step per fret. G shapes with a capo on 2 sound in A; on 3, in Bb; on 4, in B. Find your shape in the left column and read across.

The singer wants it in a different key

Work backwards: pick the key, then the capo and shapes that put it under your hands. Fewer frets up keeps the tone fuller; open-position G, C and D shapes ring the most.

Tips

  • Retune after you clamp. A capo pulls strings slightly sharp. A quick check with the capo on fixes it.
  • Two guitars? Use different shapes. One player in G shapes, the other with a capo playing D or C shapes in the same key, and the band sounds twice as wide.
  • Charts should name the sounding key. Write "Key of A (capo 2, G shapes)" so the bass and keys aren't guessing.
