Chords in the key of D

D major. Relative minor: Bm.

Two sharps. Bright and ringing on guitar, which is why so many worship and folk songs live here. Bm is the one barre chord standing between you and the whole key.

The 1, 4 and 5 (D, G, A) are the backbone. Add the 6m (Bm) and you can play most of what's on the radio. The 7° (C♯dim) mostly shows up as a passing chord, so don't lose sleep over it.

Progressions you'll actually run into

  • 1 5 6m 4 (The four-chord pop and worship progression): D · A · Bm · G
  • 1 4 5 (Three-chord rock, folk, blues and old hymns): D · G · A
  • 6m 4 1 5 (The same four chords starting on the minor): Bm · G · D · A
  • 2m 5 1 (The jazz turnaround): Em · A · D
  • 1 6m 4 5 (The fifties doo-wop changes): D · Bm · G · A

Those numbers are the Nashville number system. Learn the progression as numbers once and it works in every key.

Playing in D on guitar

No capo, D shapes; capo 2, play C shapes; capo 5, play A shapes; capo 7, play G shapes.

The capo chart has every key, and the transpose chart moves a song somewhere else entirely.

Neighbor keys

Songs that leave D usually go to its 4 or 5: key of G or key of A. Or see every key.
