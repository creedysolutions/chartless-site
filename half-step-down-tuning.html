Half step down tuning (Eb standard)

Tuning, low to high: Eb Ab Db Gb Bb Eb
Short answer: tune every string down one fret. Keep every chord shape you know. Everything sounds a half step lower: a G shape is a Gb, an E shape is an Eb.

Why bands tune down

  • Singers. A half step lower can be the difference between a high note that lands and one that strains.
  • Feel. Slightly looser strings bend easier and sound a little darker. Plenty of classic rock and blues records were cut this way.
  • Matching a record. If a song sounds in Eb or Ab and the chords feel wrong in standard, the band on the record probably tuned down.

Playing along without retuning

If you don't want to retune, play the song in standard with every chord moved down a half step (Gb instead of G, Eb instead of E). That usually means barre chords. The other way is a capo: tune down for the gig and capo 1 to get back to standard pitch when you need it. See the transpose chart and the capo chart.
