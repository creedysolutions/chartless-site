How to figure out the chords to a song by ear

Most songs use four chords out of seven. Find the key first and you're choosing between four, not twenty-four.
Short answer: find the key (where the song feels at rest), then try the 1, 4, 5 and 6m chords of that key against the bass line. Most popular and worship songs live on those four. Only go looking for anything else when none of them fits.

1. Find the key first

Play the song and hum the note that feels like home, usually the last note of the chorus or the final chord. Find that note on your instrument. That's probably your key. If the song feels sad at rest, it's a minor key; count from the relative major (A minor uses C major's chords).

2. Write down the four likely chords

For the key you found, list the 1, 4, 5 and 6m. In G that's G, C, D and Em. In D it's D, G, A and Bm. In E, E, A, B and C#m. The Nashville number chart has every key.

3. Follow the bass, not the melody

The melody wanders across every chord. The bass usually plays the chord's root. Hum the low note at each change and match it to one of your four. If the bass note fits two candidates, pick the one that sounds right on the downbeat.

4. Count the changes

Tap your foot and count to four. Most songs change chords once or twice a bar. Write one chord per bar, and mark where a chord comes early.

5. Then the odd ones

If none of the four fits, try the 2m and 3m, then a flat 7 (F in the key of G), then a major 2 (A in G). Those cover almost everything left.
